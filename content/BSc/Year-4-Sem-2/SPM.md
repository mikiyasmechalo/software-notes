# CH1 - Intro to SPM
**Project** - a temporary endeavor undertaken to accomplish a unique product or service.
- a sequence of *unique*, *complex*, and *connected* activities having one *goal* or purpose and has to be completed by a specific *time*, within *budget*, and according to *specification*. 
**Operation** - a work performed repetitively and is an ongoing process. has no "end date". it is "ongoing".

**Project Characteristics** 
- Has a goal and start & end time.
- They are customer focused and Performed by people.
- Non-routine tasks are involved
- Planning is required
- Work is carried out in several phases

**Software Project** 
It is the complete procedure of software development from requirement gathering to testing and maintenance.
*Invisibility* - Is invisible till it is ready & runs.
*Complexity* - There is inter-dependency among components. Complex to see the progress of the project.
*Conformity* - software system has to conform to the requirement of human clients.
*Flexibility* - Software is its flexible and easy to change. 

**Management** 
The Process of *planning, organizing, leading, and controlling* resources and activities within an organization to achieve specific goals efficiently and effectively.
It is a dynamic, complex, flexible, and rational process of accomplishing goals within an env.
It has the role to put together/align the *mission*, *vision*, *values*, and *culture* of the organizations with his strategy for achieving the business’s present and future goals.

**Managerial Activities** - Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Monitoring (checking on progress), Controlling (taking action to remedy hold-ups), Innovating, Representing (liaising with clients, users, developers, suppliers and other stakeholders )

**Management approaches** 
- *Traditional culture* - managers think and employees do.
- *Quality culture* - managers are coaches, they communicate, provide resources, remove barriers, seek employee feedback, build trust, provide training, reward performance.

**Management Skills** 
- *Technical Skills* - ability to apply specific knowledge to perform task or solve problems.
- *Interpersonal Skills* - ability to interact, communicate, and build relationships with others effectively. e.g. communication, conflict resolution, empathy, motivation, and team building.
- *Conceptual Skills* - The ability to understand complex situations and think strategically to solve problems and make decisions. e.g. problem solving, decision making, planning

**Project Management** 
Process of planning, executing and overseeing tasks and resources to achieve specific goals and deliverables within defined constraints, such as time, budget, and scope.
Applying knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to bring a temporary project to a completion.
Project managers strive to meet the triple constraint (*Scope, Time, and Cost*).

**Project Manager** 
A professional responsible for planning, executing, and closing projects to meet goals within constraints. 

**Software Project Management** 
The process of planning, organizing, and overseeing the development and delivery of software projects.

### Categories of Software projects
**Application Development** 
developing an application (desktop, web, mobile, embedded).
Types:
- One-offs - specifically created for a client
- Off-the-shelf - to fill the need of a large set of users
- Customized off-the-shelf - e.g. ERP

**Process and Systems Re-Engineering** 
Change how the operational work of an organization is carried out to achieve a goal like efficiency or improved quality.
 
**System Integration Services** 
Automating the information flow among the systems of an organization.
Types of integration:
- *Horizontal*: Integration of systems performing similar operations
- *Vertical*: Integration of systems automating different steps of a procedure

**Consulting Services** 
Typically asked to gain a know-how outsize a company’s core competence.

**Installation and Training Services** 
Services related to the installation or training on specific software systems.
- also a revenue model in open source development

### Project Management Tools and Techniques
- Project Charter, scope statement, and WBS (work breakdown structure) (scope)
- Gantt charts, network diagrams, critical path analysis, critical chain scheduling (time)
- Cost estimates and earned value management (cost)

Although PERT and CPM seem alike in terms of their approaches, they are different in terms of their assumptions. 
- CPM is used for projects that assume predetermined times for carrying on the planned-for activities, 
- PERT method is used for projects that assume uncertain or varied times for carrying on the activities.

**Best Practices for SPM** 
1. *Clearly Define Project Goals and Scope* - to avoid ambiguity, manage expectations, reduces the risk of scope creep.
2. *Choose the Right Methodology* - Agile, Waterfall, or Hybrid
3. *Effective Communication and Collaboration* - collaboration tools, regular meetings, and clear communication channels
4. *Continuous Risk Management* 
5. *Monitor Progress and Adapt* - Flexibility is crucial to keeping the project moving forward without compromising quality.
6. *Prioritize Quality Assurance* - throughout the project, not just at the end. testing, code reviews, and quality checks.
7. *Comprehensive Documentation* - technical documentation, meeting notes, decisions, and changes in scope.
8. *Utilizing Project Management Tools* - Leveraging tools like Jira, Trello, or Asana to streamline task management, collaboration, and progress tracking.

### Projects, Programs, and Portfolios
**Project** - A temporary endeavor to create a unique product, service or result. with specific objectives and constraints, such as scope, time, and budget. 
- is unique and not a part of regular business operations. 
- Successful if completed: on time, within budget, meeting specs, and ensuring customer satisfaction.

**Program** - a group of related projects managed in a coordinated manner, which offers greater value than managing them individually.
- focuses on achieving strategic objectives and delivering benefits that contribute to the organization’s wider goals.
- Successful if all integral components (projects/programs) are successful.
*Example*: developing a new app, upgrading the online shopping platform, and implementing a new CRM system, all to enhance customer experience.

**Portfolio** - a collection of projects, programs, subsidiary portfolios, and operations managed as a group to achieve strategic objectives, managed at the top level (PMO).
- a portfolio is not necessarily a group of related work. 
- It is organized based on achieving specific strategic business objectives.
- Successful if all integral components (programs/projects/portfolios) are successful.

##### Project Initiation Phase
Involves defining the purpose & objectives of a project.
- Conducting initial feasibility studies 
- Develop a project charter
- Identifying stakeholders
- Justifying the business case

**Feasibility Study** 
Conducted to determine the practicality and viability of a proposed project.
It determines the practicality by assessing the opportunities and threats of the proposed plan.
Asks three questions:
1. Why do it? The benefits.
2. Can it be done? Technically, Financial/Economical, Operational Feasibility, Legal Feasibility, Scheduling Feasibility, Market Feasibility, Organization Feasibility 
3. Who should be involved? stakeholders

*Operational Feasibility*: evaluates how well the proposed project aligns with the organization’s operational capabilities and processes.
*Market Feasibility*: Analyses the market demand for the product or service being proposed.
*Organizational feasibility*: if a project, system, or business venture can be successfully implemented, accepted, within an organization's existing structure, resources, and culture.

**Project Charter** 
Document issued by the project initiator or sponsor that formally authorizes the existence of a project and provides the project manager with the authority to apply organizational resources to project activities.
Must be signed by a project sponsor, as it provides explicit permission to begin project work and utilize organizational resources, from team members and financing to technology and software. 
How is a charter written? ->
```
Decide on a project name.
Determine the project's aim, goal, and specifications.
Establish a spending plan.
Present a Business Case.
Describe the deliverables.
Evaluate the risks and scope.
Establish a timeline or goals.
Enumerate the important parties (stakehoders).
Note Key Assumptions & Constraints
Assign roles and duties to the team.
```

**Project Stakeholders** 
1. *Project Manager*: is the team leader and is responsible for ensuring that all of the project management and technical development processes are in place and are being carried out within a set of specific requirements, defined processes, and quality standards.
2. *Project Sponsor* - provides resources and support for the project and is accountable for enabling success. may be external or internal to the project manager’s organization.
3. *Subject Matter Expert(s)* (SME) - who has specific knowledge, expertise, or insight in a specific functional area needed to support the project.
4. *Technical Expert*(s) (TE) - include systems analysts, network specialists, programmers, graphic artists, trainers, and so forth.
5. *Support staff* - include such roles as contracting, financial management, logistics, legal, safety, etc.
6. *Users* 
7. *Suppliers* 

##### Project Planning
Consists of estimating the following attributes of the project: 
- *Project size*: What will be problem complexity in terms of the effort and time required? 
- *Cost*: How much is it going to cost to develop the project? 
- *Duration*: How long is it going to take to complete development? 
- *Effort*: How much effort would be required? 

There are four basic reasons for project planning:
1. To eliminate or reduce uncertainty
2. To improve efficiency of the operation
3. To obtain a better understanding of the objectives
4. To provide a basis for monitoring and controlling work.

Project plans must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic and Timely).

**Key Outputs of planning phase** 
- Create WBS, WBS Dictionary
- Develop Schedule
- Develop Budget
- Determine roles & Responsibilities
- Develop Risk Plan
- Create Performance Measurement baselines
- Develop Preliminary Scope Statement
- Test Plan, etc.

##### Project Execution
The actions necessary to complete the work described in the planning activities. 
Requires the most amount of time since much of the work is done in this timeframe.
It involves 
- Coordinating people & other resources to carry out the various plans. 
- Create products, services, or results of the project/phase. 
- Directing & managing project work, managing project knowledge, acquiring resources, & conducting procurements.

Project managers:
- oversee a team
- manage budget and resources
- communicate to stakeholders, etc.

Key Outputs of this phase
- Signoff that key milestones are complete
- Documented performance reports

##### Project Monitoring and Control
Regularly measuring and monitoring progress to ensure that the project team meets the project objectives. 
- Tracking key performance indicators
- Managing changes and addressing issues if they arise, etc.

Things you could measure include, for example:
- if your project is on schedule and budget
- if specific tasks are being completed
- if issues are adequately addressed

Key outputs of this phase are:
- Receive all signoffs that work is completed as per requirements
- Archive all project assets 
- Create and document lessons learned 

##### Project Close
Project closure often involves things like:
- Finalizing all activities
- Document final performance reporting
- Handing over the deliverables
- Releasing staff and resources
- Archiving or handing over any relevant project documents
- Cancelling supplier contracts
- Completion of all activities across the project
- Preparing the final project budget and report

Key outputs of this phase are:
- Receive all signoffs that work is completed as per requirements
- Index & archive all project assets
- Create and document lessons learned

# CH 2 - Pt.2 Agile Software Project Management
### Agile Project Management (APM)
- an iterative and flexible methodology that emphasizes adaptability, collaboration, customer feedback, and customer satisfaction. 
- Breaks projects into smaller sprints for faster and flexible delivery.

Benefits of APM
- Customer satisfaction
- adaptability to change
- faster time to market
- enhanced collaboration and communication
- improved project visibility
- early identification issues

**Phases of APM** 
1. *Envision* - define the project vision and overall project objectives. Identify stakeholders, scope, and business goals. key activities:-
	- create Project charter/ vision statement
	- define product goals and target users
	- choose agile framework
2. *Speculate* - break down the vision into prioritized features and create a release roadmap.
	- create product backlog
	- define epics and user stories
	- estimate story points
	- prioritize backlog using (e.g. `MoSCoW` method)
3. *Explore/ Execution* - execute iterations to develop working product increments.
	- conduct sprint planning
	- develop, code, test user stories
	- daily scrum meetings
	- continuous integration and testing
	- deliver working software each sprint
4. *Adapt/ review* - review feedback from users and stakeholders and adjust goals as needed.
	- sprint review meetings
	- collect feedback
	- update backlog based on feedback
5. *Close* - finalize documentation and conduct a retrospective for lessons learned
	- release working increment to users 
	- sprint retrospective
	- document learnings and adjust process

### Agile Project Management (APM) Frameworks
- **Scrum**: Uses time-boxed iterations called sprints to deliver working software at regular intervals.
- **Kanban**: Visualizes work in progress through a board with columns representing different stages of work. 
- **Lean**: Focuses in reducing waste and maximizing efficiency through continuous improvement.
- **Extreme**: Programming: Emphasizes pair programming and code review to improve code quality.
- **Crystal**: offers flexible approach that can be tailored to the specific needs of a project of team. 

##### Scrum
**Scrum Features**
* *Iterative and Incremental Sprints*: 2-4 week cycles producing shippable product increments.
* *Defined Roles*: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team.
* *Key Artifacts*: Product Backlog (master list of requirements), Sprint Backlog (tasks for the current Sprint), and the Increment (the usable, working product).
* *Regular Ceremonies*: Sprint Planning, Daily Standup, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.

Scrum is built on the idea that complex problems can’t be fully understood upfront. Instead, it relies on transparency, inspection, and adaptation to navigate uncertainties.

**Scrum Advantage** 
- Faster Time-to-Market
- Customer-Centric - higher customer satisfaction and better product-market fit.
- Improved Collaboration - promotes collaboration among team members, stakeholders, and customers

##### Kanban
**Kanban Principles** 
* *Visualizing Work*: Uses visual boards to show workflow, item status, and bottlenecks.
* *Limiting Work in Progress (WIP):* Sets WIP limits to prevent overload and ensure a steady pace.
* *Managing Flow*: Promotes continuous and smooth work item flow, minimizing delays.
* *Explicit Process Policies*: Clearly defines workflow rules for consistency.

**Kanban Workflow Phases**
* *To-Do:* The initial list of all tasks/user stories, with brief descriptions and details.
* *In-Progress*: Tasks currently under active development.
* *Validation*: Code review, various testing (unit, acceptance, system), and bug rectification.
* *Done*: Finished and deployed tasks that have passed all tests and quality assurance.

**Benefits of Kanban** 
- *Enhanced Visibility*: real-time visibility into work status, allowing for quick identification of blockers and opportunities for improvement.
- *Increased Flexibility*: Kanban’s adaptability makes it suitable for various industries and processes, from software development to customer support.
- *Continuous Improvement*: Kanban fosters a culture of continuous improvement by encouraging teams to regularly review and optimize their workflows.

**Kanban vs Scrum** 

| Feature         | Kanban                                                                                    | Scrum                                                                                         |
| :-------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Team Roles**  | No specific roles; flexible. Optional: Service Delivery Manager, Service Request Manager. | Defined roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team.                                 |
| **Planning**    | Continuous, just-in-time planning based on workflow data.                                 | Occurs at the start of each sprint; work broken down.                                         |
| **Commitment**  | Based on team capacity; focus on completing current tasks.                                | Teams commit to a defined amount of work for each sprint.                                     |
| **Key Metrics** | Lead time, cycle time (cumulative flow diagrams, cycle time histograms).                  | Velocity, sprint progress (burndown charts, velocity charts).                                 |

| **Work Boards** | Continuous board, never reset; tasks flow through stages.                                 | Board resets at the end of each sprint; new work planned.                                     |
| **Estimation**  | Based on historical data to predict delivery.                                             | During backlog refinement; determines sprint capacity.                                        |

# CH 3 - WBS and Schedule

### Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
- WBS development is the first major step in the planning process after requirements are defined.
- “The WBS represents a logical decomposition of the work to be performed and focuses on how the product, service, or result is naturally subdivided. It is an outline of what work is to be performed.”  `Gregory T. Haugan (2002)` 
- It is the process of subdividing project deliverables and project work into smaller, more manageable components.

The project manager must structure the work into small elements that are:
- *Manageable*, specific authority and responsibility assigned
- *Independent*, or with minimum interfacing and dependence on other ongoing elements
- *Integrable* so that the total package can be seen
- *Measurable* in terms of progress

- Project-task scheduling involves deciding which tasks would be taken up when.
- Developing the WBS Should Involve the People Who Will Be Doing the Work.
- Each descending level of the WBS represents an increasingly detailed definition of the project work.
- Should contain activities that are short in duration (between 8 and 80 hours). 

WBS helps to:
- Identify the main activities
- An instrument for tracking costs and work performance.
- Provides a coordinating framework for the various parts of a project.
- Defines authority and responsibilities for the details of the project.
- Identifies “work packages”.

**WBS Package** / work package
- is the lowest and most detailed level of work. It represents a manageable, specific unit of work that can be clearly defined, estimated, assigned, and tracked.
- In the context of the WBS, work refers to work products or deliverables that are the result of activity and not to the activity itself.
- Work that doesn’t fit into the WBS does not fit within the project.
- Work packages provide a logical basis for defining the project activities and assigning resources.

**WBS Techniques** 
Codes assigned to each WBS component
- Level 0 - project itself
- Level 1 - major deliverables
- Level 2 - individual components of each deliverable
- ...
- Final level - work package

**Approach to Develop WBS** 
*Phase based approach* (normal)
Involves structuring the project work according to the major phases or stages of the project lifecycle. and the deliverables and activities within each phase are further broken down. It provides a chronological organization of work.
Advantage
- Activity list likely complete and non-overlapping
- WBS gives a structure that can be refined as the project proceeds

Disadvantage
- May miss some activities related to final product

*Product based approach* 
Product Breakdown Structure (PBS)
- Shows how a system can be broken down into different products for development
Consider building a new computer. A product-based WBS would be:
1.  *CPU Unit*
	* Processor
	* Cooling system
2.  *Motherboard Assembly*
	* Motherboard
	* RAM modules
3.  *Storage Devices*
	* SSD
	* HDD
4.  *Graphics System*
	* Graphics card
	* Monitor

*Hybrid approach* 
- A mix of the phase-based and product based approaches (most commonly used)
- The WBS consists of a list of the products of the project; and a list of phases for each product

For developing a new mobile application, a hybrid WBS might be:
1.  **Phase: Planning**
	* Product: Requirements Document
	* Product: Project Plan
2.  **Phase: Design**
	* Product: User Interface Mockups
	* Product: Database Schema
3.  **Phase: Development**
	* Product: iOS App Module A
	* Product: iOS App Module B
	* Product: Android App Module A
	* Product: Android App Module B
	* Product: Backend API
4.  **Phase: Testing**
	* Product: Test Cases
	* Product: Bug Report Log


**Deliverables versus Milestones** 
**Deliverables** 
- Tangible, verifiable work products
- Reports, presentations, prototypes, etc.

**Milestones** 
- Significant events or achievements
- Acceptance of deliverables or phase completion
- Cruxes (proof of concepts)
- Quality control 
- Keeps team focused 

**How do you develop a good WBS?** 
*Top down approach:* 
- Start at the highest, top level activities and systematically develop increasing levels of detail for all activities
Good if
- You (or your team) are familiar with the problem.
- You have successfully managed a similar project in the past.
- You are not introducing new methodologies or tools.

*Bottom up approach (“Brainstorming”):* 
- Generate all activities you can think of that will have to be done and then group them into categories

*Analogy Approach* 
- Use a similar project’s WBS as a starting point.

**WBS Dictionary** 
- It is a document that provides detailed information about each WBS item.
- Use a short paragraph describing each work package. also might need 1 or more pages.

##### Scheduling
Scheduling is created based on the WBS by identifying the sequence of activities as well as the interdependencies and relationships.
The project schedule shows two things:
- The sequence in which the work will be carried out.
- The dates at which we plan activities to start and finish.

Several tools and techniques assist in project network development:
- Project network Diagrams
- Activity on the Arrow(AOA)/Activity on the Node(AON), 
- Critical path method/Critical path analysis, and
- PERT (Program Evaluation and Review Technique)
- Gantt charts 

**Activity on Arrow (AOA)** 
- It is a tool that graphically represents all of the project activities and tasks,  their logical sequence and dependencies.
- Activities are represented by arrows and connected at points called nodes.
- Node is simply the starting and ending point of an activity. 

*Rules on AOA* 
- Construction begins with the activities and tasks (should have an associated time estimate) that were defined in the WBS
- Determine which activities are predecessors, successors, or parallel
- AOA flows from left to right
- An activity cannot begin until all of its predecessor activities have been completed

*Steps on Creating AOA Diagrams* 
1. Find all of the activities that start at node 1.  
2. Draw their consecutive nodes and draw arrows between node 1 and the consecutive nodes.  
3. Put the activity letter or name and duration estimate on the associated arrow.
4. Continue drawing the network diagram, working from left to right.  
5. Look for bursts and merges.  Bursts occur when a single node is followed by two or more activities.  A merge occurs when two or more nodes precede a single node.
6. Continue drawing the project network diagram until all activities are included on the diagram that have dependencies
7. As a rule of thumb, all arrowheads should face toward the right, and no arrows should cross on an AOA network diagram

e.g.

| Tasks | Duration(days) | Predecessors |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------ |
| A     | 2              | ---          |
| B     | 5              | A            |
| C     | 4              | B            |
| D     | 3              | B            |
| E     | 1              | B            |
| F     | 4              | C, D         |
| G     | 3              | D, E         |
| H     | 2              | F, G         |
| I     | 5              | G            |

##### CPM - Critical Path Method
It is a network diagramming technique used to predict the project duration.
It identifies the longest path through the network diagram and has the least amount of slack or float.

Float (slack) - amount of time that a task can be delayed without causing a delay to:
- the early start date of subsequent tasks (free float)
- project completion date (total float)

Critical event - an event that has zero slack
Critical path concerned only with the time dimension of a project (critical to the project's end date), does not includes the most critical activities.
A project can have multiple critical paths.

Primary objectives:
- Plan for the fastest completion of the project
- Identify activities whose delays is likely to affect the completion date for the whole project

Benefit of Critical Path Analysis:
- During planning stage: shortening the critical path will reduce the overall project duration.
- During management stage: Pay more attention to those activities which fall in the critical path.

##### Two-pass Method
Used to determine the amount of slack each activity has.
Make two logical passes through the constructed network: The forward and the backward pass 

*The forward pass* 
This pass helps determine the **earliest possible times** activities can start and finish.

* *Goal*: Find Early Start (ES) and Early Finish (EF) for each task.
* *Starting Point:*
  * For the **first task**, **ES** = Project Start Date.
* For all other tasks:
  * *Early Start (ES)*: The *highest* Early Finish (EF) of all its immediate predecessors.
    * `ES = Max(EF of all immediate predecessors)` 
  * *Early Finish (EF)*: The earliest time a task can finish.
    * `EF = ES + Duration` 


*The backward pass* 
This pass helps determine the **latest possible times** activities can start and finish without delaying the overall project.

* *Goal*: Find Late Start (LS) and Late Finish (LF) for each task.
* *Starting Point*:
	* For the **last task**, **LF** = its Early Finish (EF) (calculated in the Forward Pass).
*  For all other tasks (working backward from the end):
  * *Late Finish (LF)*: The *lowest* Late Start (LS) of all its immediate successors.
    * `LF = Min(LS of all immediate successors)`
  * *Late Start (LS)*: The latest time a task can begin without delaying the project.
    * `LS = LF - Duration` 


**Calculating Slack Time** 
Slack: amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project
 - Activity slack = LS - ES = LF - EF
Critical activities: have zero slack and lie on a critical path.
Activities with no slack or very little slack need to be scheduled very carefully.

##### Project Evaluation Review Technique (PERT)
- a PM tool used to calculate the amount of time it will take to realistically finish a project.
- PERT applies the critical path method (CPM) to a weighted average duration estimate.

Create 3 estimates of time to complete each activity (most likely time, Optimistic time and Pessimistic time), instead of one specific estimate, as CPM does 
- Most likely Time (MLT) - is based on the duration of the activity, given the resources likely to be assigned, their productivity, realistic expectations of availability for the activity, dependencies on other participants, and interruptions
- Optimistic Time (OT) - minimum time in which an activity or task can be completed, where everything goes well.
- Pessimistic Time (PT) - worst-case scenario and is viewed as the maximum time in which an activity can or should be completed

##### Gantt Chart
Gantt charts illustrate the start and finish dates of the work elements (tasks) of a project.
Graph or bar chart with a bar for each project activity that shows passage of time

Elements of a Gantt chart:
- Task names
- Start and finish dates of each tasks (graphically)
- Dependency relationships
- Task duration in an additional column
- Lag relationships (start-to-start, finish-to-start etc.)
- Name of the project worker responsible for the task or
- Resource specifications


# CH 4 - Software Project Estimation
Estimation is attempt to determine how much money, effort, resources & time it will take to build a specific system or project.
Proper project planning and control is not possible without a sound and reliable estimate.
Both under and over-estimation are bad for the project and the organization.

Estimation includes:
- Project size 
- Cost
- Duration 
- Effort

### Estimation Techniques 
These estimates not only help in quoting the project cost, resource planning and scheduling. 
There are several estimation techniques:
- Guesstimating
- Delphi
- Analogy
- top-down
- bottom-up

**Metrics for Software Project Size Estimation** 
The two popular metrics to estimate a size: 
- Lines of code (LOC), 
- Function point (FP) and

*LOC* 
- project size is estimated by counting the number of source instructions in the developed program. comments and headers are ignored.
- In order to estimate the LOC count at the beginning of a project, project managers usually divide the problem into modules
- Each module into sub modules and so on, until the sizes of the different leaf-level modules can be approximately predicted. 

Advantages of LOC:
- Universally accepted and used in models like COCOMO.
- Estimation is closer to the developer's perspective.
- At project completion, LOC is easily quantified.
- It has a specific connection to the result.
- Simple to use.

Shortcoming of LOC:
- Different programming languages contain a different number of lines.
- No proper industry standard exists for this technique.
- It is difficult to estimate the size using this technique in the early stages of the project.
- When platforms and languages are different, LOC cannot be used to normalize.

*Function Points* 
Function points are a synthetic metric, similar to hours, & kilos.
Function points focus on the functionality and complexity of an application system or a particular module.
The conceptual idea behind the function point metric is that the size of a software product is directly dependent on the number of different functions or features it supports. 

Each function when invoked reads some input data and transforms it to the corresponding output data. 

*Functional Points Analysis* 
Function point analysis is based on an evaluation of five data and transactional types that define the application boundary:
- *Internal Logical File (ILF)* - is a logical file that can be maintained (updated/added/deleted) inside the application boundary.
- *External Interface File (EIF)* - is referenced (read-only) by the application but maintained by another application system.
- *External Input (EI)* - refers to processes data from outside to inside the boundary to maintain (add, change, delete) Internal Logical Files (ILFs).
- *External Output (EO)* - Processes data from inside to outside the boundary, with a focus on presenting derived information, such as reports or calculated data, to the user.
- *External Inquiry (EQ)* - It is the number of distinct interactive queries which can be made by the users. such as print-account-balance

**Calculating Function Points (FP)** 
*Step 1: Calculate the Unadjusted Function Point (UFP)* 
To find the UFP, you must identify, categorize, and count all functional components of the system. Each component is assigned a complexity rating (Low, Average, or High) based on its data requirements and internal processing.

*UFP Formula:* 
$$UFP = \sum (\text{Count of component type} \times \text{Weighting factor})$$
*Weighting Table* 

| Functional Component              | Low | Average | High |
| --------------------------------- | --- | ------- | ---- |
| **External Input (EI)**           | 3   | 4       | 6    |
| **External Output (EO)**          | 4   | 5       | 7    |
| **External Inquiry (EQ)**         | 3   | 4       | 6    |
| **Internal Logical File (ILF)**   | 7   | 10      | 15   |
| **External Interface File (EIF)** | 5   | 7       | 10   |
*Step 2: Calculate the Final Function Point (FP)*
The UFP is a raw count. To account for the system's technical and operational environment, we apply a Value Adjustment Factor (VAF).
1. **Determine the Degree of Influence (DI):** Evaluate 14 General System Characteristics (e.g., data communications, performance, complexity) on a scale of 0 to 5.
2. **Calculate VAF:** 
$$VAF = 0.65 + (0.01 \times \sum DI)$$
3. **Calculate Final FP:** 
$$FP = UFP \times VAF$$

**Example Calculation** 
Imagine a small system with the following components identified:

| Component | Complexity | Count | Calculation | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **External Input** | Low | 2 | $2 \times 3$ | 6 |
| **External Output** | Average | 3 | $3 \times 5$ | 15 |
| **Internal Logical File** | High | 1 | $1 \times 15$ | 15 |
| **Total UFP** |  |  |  | **36** |
**Assuming a Total Degree of Influence (DI) of 35:** 
1. **Calculate VAF:** 
$$VAF = 0.65 + (0.01 \times 35) = 0.65 + 0.35 = 1.00$$

2. **Calculate Final FP:** 
$$FP = 36 \times 1.00 = 36$$

In this example, the system has a final value of **36 Function Points**. If the DI were higher (indicating a more complex, high-performance, or highly distributed system), the VAF would increase, resulting in a higher total FP count.

### COCOMO (Constructive Cost Model)
Based on LOC estimates, it is used to estimate cost, effort, and schedule.
It predicts project effort, time, and team size based on software size (KLOC) and complexity, aiding in project planning, risk management, and resource allocation.

In COCOMO, projects are categorized into three types:
- Organic project
	- small projects (2-50 KLOC)
	- developed with experienced developers
	- a familiar project
	- minimal innovation required
	- $E = 2.4(50)^{1.05}$ 

- Semi-detached projects 
	- Medium size projects (50-300 KLOC),
	- Complexity not too high and not too low. 
	- Developed with experienced and inexperienced developers in team, 
	- $E = 3.0(250)^{1.12}$ 

- Embedded projects
	- Large projects with fixed requirement of resources (more than 300 KLOC), 
	- Larger team with little previous experience. 
	- High level of complexity, creativity and experience requirement.
	- Example: ATM, Air Traffic Control, Banking Software
	- $E = 3.6(400)^{1.20}$ 

##### Basic COCOMO Model
Estimates the software development effort quickly and roughly
It considers the number of lines of code in project.
- $𝐸𝑓𝑓𝑜𝑟𝑡 (𝐸)=𝑎∗(𝐾𝐿𝑂𝐶)^𝑏$ in Man-months
- $𝑆𝑐𝑒𝑑𝑢𝑙𝑒 𝑇𝑖𝑚𝑒 (𝐷)=𝑐(𝐸)^𝑑$ in months

Where
- a, b, c, d = the constraint parameters for a software project 

| Project Type | a   | b    | c   | d    |
| ------------ | --- | ---- | --- | ---- |
| Organic      | 2.4 | 1.05 | 2.5 | 0.38 |
| Semidetached | 3   | 1.12 | 2.5 | 0.35 |
| Embedded     | 3.6 | 1.2  | 2.5 | 0.32 |
**Example:** 
Consider software project using semi-detached mode with 300 KLOC. Find out Effort estimation, Development and Person estimation. 
*Solution* 
**Effort (E):** 
$$E = a \times (KLOC)^b$$
$$E = 3 \times (300)^{1.12} = 1,784.42 \text{ PM}$$

**Schedule Time (D):** $$D = c \times (E)^d$$ $$D = 2.5 \times (1,784.42)^{0.35} = 34.35 \text{ Months}$$
**Persons Required (P):** 
$$P = E / D$$
$$P = 1,784.42 / 34.35 = 51.95 \approx 52 \text{ Persons}$$


##### Intermediate COCOMO Model
Extension of basic COCOMO model which enhances more accuracy to cost estimation model result.
It includes cost drivers (Product, Hardware, Resources & Project Parameter) of projects.
**Effort (E):**
$$E = a \times (KLOC)^b \times EAF \text{ MM}$$
**Schedule Time (D):**
$$D = c \times (E)^d \text{ Months (M)}$$
- EAF = Effort Adjustment Factor, which is calculated by multiplying the parameter values of cost deriver parameters. For ideal, the value is 1.

##### Detailed COCOMO Model
Incorporates all qualities of both Basic and Intermediate strategies on each software engineering process.
The whole software is divided into different modules and apply COCOMO in different ways.
The six phases of detailed COCOMO are

# CH 5 - Risk Management

- PMBOK defines project risk as: an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more project objectives such as scope, schedule, cost, and quality.

Conceptual definition of risk
- Risk concerns future happenings
- Risk involves change in mind, opinion, actions, places, etc.
- Risk involves choice and the uncertainty that choice entails

Risk is generally caused due to lack of information, control or time.
Risk Management is an attempt to minimize the chances of failure caused by unplanned events.

**Software Risk** 
A possibility of suffering from loss in software development process. (increase in production cost, development of poor quality software, not being able to complete the project on time).

**Types of Software Risk** 1
- **Internal risks** that are within the control of the project manager. 
- **External risks** that are beyond the control of project manager. 

**Types of Software Risk** 2
*Technical Risks* 
- Include problems with languages, project size, project functionality, platforms, methods, standards, or processes. 
- These risks may result from excessive constraints, lack of experience, poorly defined parameters, or dependencies on organizations outside the direct control of the project team.

*Management Risks* 
- Include lack of planning, lack of management experience and training, communications problems, organizational issues, lack of authority, and control problems.

*Financial risks* 
- Include cash flow, capital and budgetary issues, and return on investment constraints.

*Contractual and legal risks* 
- Include changing requirements, market-driven schedules, health & safety issues, government regulation, and product warranty issues.

*Personnel risks* 
- Include staffing lags, experience and training problems, ethical and moral issues, staff conflicts, and productivity issues.

**Types of Software Risk** 3
- *Known risks* - Those risks that can be uncovered after careful evaluation of the project plan, the business and technical environment in which the project is being developed (e.g., unrealistic delivery date)
- *Predictable risks* - Those risks that are extrapolated from past project experience (e.g., past turnover)
- *Unpredictable risks* - Those risks that can and do occur, but are extremely difficult to identify in advance

### Software Risk Management (SRM)
The systematic process of identifying, analyzing, and responding to project risk.
Maximizing the probability and consequences of positive events and minimizing the probability and consequences of adverse events.
Emphasis is shifted from “crisis management" to "anticipatory management”.

**Reasons for Implementing SRM** 
1. Avoiding software project disasters - run away budgets and schedules, defect-ridden software products, and operational failures.
2. Avoiding rework caused by erroneous, missing, or ambiguous requirements, design or code.
3. Avoiding overkill with detection and prevention techniques in area of minimal or no risk.
4. Stimulating a win-win software solution where the customer receives the product they need and the vendor makes the profits they expect.

**Reactive vs. Proactive Risk Strategies** 
*Reactive risk strategies:* 
- The majority of software teams and managers rely on this approach.
- Nothing is done about risks until something goes wrong
	- The team then flies into action in an attempt to correct the problem rapidly (fire fighting).
- Crisis management is the choice of management techniques.

*Proactive risk strategies:* 
- Steps for risk management are followed.
- Primary objective is to avoid risk and to have a contingency plan in place to handle unavoidable risks in a controlled and effective manner.

##### Risk Management Processes
- **Risk Management Planning** - deciding how to approach and plan the risk management activities for the project
	- output - *Risk management plan*

	- Expected Value - EV of a payoff table is the weighted average of all possible outcomes (profits or losses) used for a decision.
	- The Expected Value (EV) is calculated by summing the products of each possible outcome and its respective probability: $$EV = \sum (Outcome \times Probability)$$
	- Decision Trees
	- Risk Impact Table
	- Tusler’s risk classification scheme
- **Quantitative Risk Analysis** - numerically estimating the effects of risks on project objectives
	- Discrete Probability Distributions
		- Binomial
	- Continuous Probability Distributions
		- Normal
		- PERT
		- Triangular
	- Simulations
- **Risk Response Planning** - taking steps to enhance opportunities and reduce threats to meeting project objectives
	- The four basic strategies for managing *negative risks* are:
	* **Risk Avoidance:** Eliminating the threat by removing its root causes.
	* **Risk Acceptance:** Acknowledging the consequences; this can be active (creating contingency plans) or passive (accepting the outcome).
	* **Risk Transference:** Shifting the responsibility and consequences of the risk to a third party.
	* **Risk Mitigation:** Reducing the likelihood or impact of a risk through proactive measures like using proven technology or hiring competent personnel.
	* Basic response strategies for *positive risks*.
	- *Risk exploitation* - doing whatever you can to make sure the positive risk happens
	- *Risk sharing* - allocating ownership of the risk to another party
	- *Risk enhancement* 
	- *Risk acceptance* 

- **Risk Monitoring and Control** - monitoring identified and residual risks, identifying new risks, carrying out risk response plans, and evaluating the effectiveness of risk strategies throughout the life of the project

*Nominal Group Technique (NGT)* 
1. Each individual silently writes her or his ideas on a piece of paper
2. Each idea is then written on a board or flip chart one at a time in a round-robin fashion until each individual has listed all of his or her ideas. 
3. The group then discusses and clarifies each of the ideas.
4. Each individual then silently ranks and prioritizes the ideas. 
5. The group then discusses the rankings and priorities of the ideas. 
6. Each individual ranks and prioritizes the ideas again. 
7. The rankings and prioritizations are then summarized for the group. 

*Delphi Technique* 
It is a method for gathering information and opinions from a group of experts without them needing to confront each other. 
It helps to collect honest and unbiased feedback because no one knows who said what.
How does it work? 
- Prepare and send series of questionnaires of the potential risk of the project to a panel of experts. 
- Collect the distributed questionnaire from the respondents. 
- Compile the answers, summarize them, This goes on until the answers start to show a pattern or consensus.

*Cause and Effect Diagram* /fishbone /Ishikawa
- Identify the risk in terms of a threat or opportunity.
- Identify the 6M's (Main) categories  that can cause the risk to occur. 
- Identify detailed factors for each of the main factors. 
- Continue refining the diagram until satisfied that the diagram is complete.

The 6M’s stand for:
- *Materials* - parts, ingredients, supplies
- *Machinery*  - production-related equipment, materials handling equipment, software.
- *Methods*  - procedures, techniques, processes, regulations.
- *Measurement* - key indicators, measurement devices, key data capture or collection points
- *Manpower* - people and human resources, with their associated training, skills, competences
- *Mother Nature*  - environment and externalities


*Risk Register* 
Key Elements of a Risk Register
- *Risk ID & Description*: Unique identifier and detailed scenario.
- *Category*: Type of risk (e.g., technical, operational, financial).
- *Likelihood & Impact*: Probability of occurrence and severity of consequences (often rated low/medium/high or numerically).
- *Risk Rating*: Prioritization score to determine focus.
- *Risk Owner*: Person responsible for monitoring and managing the risk.
- *Mitigation Plan*: Specific actions to reduce likelihood or impact.

*Decision Trees* 
Example:
You are planning an activity which requires the use of a crane. You can rent either a **small** or **large** crane. The small crane costs less to rent (**$10,000 versus $16,000 for the large crane**) but will be slower.
As currently planned, the activity is **not on the critical path**; however, an analysis has indicated that there is a **30% probability** that the activity will become critical. In this case, **each day's delay** in the activity will cost the project **$5,000**.

The table below indicates the delay probabilities (short, medium, or long) and their respective delays associated with the small or large crane.

| Delay  | Small Crane Probability | Small Crane Delay (Days) | Large Crane Probability | Large Crane Delay (Days) |
| ------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Long   | 0.2                     | 10                       | 0.2                     | 3                        |
| Medium | 0.5                     | 5                        | 0.5                     | 2                        |
| Short  | 0.3                     | 2                        | 0.3                     | 1                        |
*Question:* 
Using **expected monetary values (EMV)** with a **risk-neutral attitude**, which crane should the project manager choose?

*Answer* 
To determine which crane the project manager should choose using Expected Monetary Value (EMV), we must calculate the total expected cost for both options, including the base rental cost and the expected cost of potential delays.
1. Define Variables
* **Rental Costs:** Small = $\$10,000$, Large = $\$16,000$
* **Probability of becoming critical:** $0.30$ 
* **Cost per day of delay:** $\$5,000$ 

2. Calculate Expected Delay Days
The expected delay is calculated as: $E(\text{Delay}) = \sum (\text{Probability} \times \text{Delay in Days})$ 

* **Small Crane Expected Delay:** 
$$(0.2 \times 10) + (0.5 \times 5) + (0.3 \times 2) = 2 + 2.5 + 0.6 = \mathbf{5.1 \text{ days}}$$

* **Large Crane Expected Delay:** 
$$(0.2 \times 3) + (0.5 \times 2) + (0.3 \times 1) = 0.6 + 1.0 + 0.3 = \mathbf{1.9 \text{ days}}$$

3. Calculate Total Expected Monetary Value (EMV)
The formula for total EMV is:
$$\text{EMV} = \text{Rental Cost} + (\text{Probability of critical path} \times \text{Expected Delay} \times \text{Cost per day})$$

* **EMV (Small Crane):** 
$$\$10,000 + (0.30 \times 5.1 \text{ days} \times \$5,000)$$
$$\$10,000 + (0.30 \times \$25,500) = \$10,000 + \$7,650 = \mathbf{\$17,650}$$
* **EMV (Large Crane):** 
$$\$16,000 + (0.30 \times 1.9 \text{ days} \times \$5,000)$$
$$\$16,000 + (0.30 \times \$9,500) = \$16,000 + \$2,850 = \mathbf{\$18,850}$$

Comparing the two results:
* Small Crane EMV: **$17,650** 
* Large Crane EMV: **$18,850** 

The project manager should choose the **Small Crane**, as it has a lower expected monetary value (total cost) of **$17,650**.

*Tusler’s Risk Classification Scheme* 
Any risk has 2 characteristics:
1) *Probability* is the likelihood that the risk will affect the project.
2) *Impact* is the effect of the risk on the project or consequences for the project.

Risks are categorized into four quadrants based on probability and impact:
* **High Probability, High Impact:** Frequent, severe risks requiring immediate attention.
* **High Probability, Low Impact:** Frequent, minor risks to be managed routinely.
* **Low Probability, High Impact:** Rare, severe risks requiring contingency planning.
* **Low Probability, Low Impact:** Rare, minor risks to be monitored or accepted.

#####  The Capability Maturity Model (CMM)
- *Software Process*: A set of activities, methods, and practices for developing and maintaining software.
- *Software Process Capability*: The expected results attainable by following a particular software process.
- *Software Process Performance*: The actual results achieved when a software process is implemented.
- *Software Process Maturity*: The level to which a software process is explicitly defined, managed, measured, controlled, and effectively utilized within an organization.

It consists of five maturity levels: initial, repeatable, defined, managed, and optimizing. 
But First:
*Immature Software Organization:* 
* Characterized by a reactive, "fire-fighting" approach, leading to exceeded schedules and budgets, and compromised functionality and quality.
* Success is heavily dependent on individual team members, with no clear basis for judging quality or improving processes.

*Mature Software Organization:* 
* Operates proactively with disciplined, well-defined, and consistently applied software processes and roles.
* Continuously improves processes based on experience and monitors the quality of each process, resulting in predictable products and processes across projects.
* Achieves more realistic budgets and schedules, increasing the likelihood of meeting project goals and objectives.

**The 5 levels:** 
*Level 1: Initial* 
* **Characteristics:** Ad hoc, reactive, and unstable software processes; project success relies on individuals, not defined processes.
* **Key Process Areas:** None.

*Level 2: Repeatable* 
* **Characteristics:** Basic project management policies, processes, and controls are established, allowing successful practices to be repeated.
* **Key Process Areas:** Software Configuration Management, Software Quality Assurance, Software Subcontract Management, Software Project Tracking and Oversight, Software Project Planning, Requirements Management.

*Level 3: Defined* 
* **Characteristics:** Standardized and documented software engineering and management processes are implemented across the organization.
* **Key Process Areas:** Peer Reviews, Intergroup Coordination, Software Product Engineering, Integrated Software Management, Training Programs, Organization Process Definition, Organization Process Focus.

*Level 4: Managed* 
* **Characteristics:** Quantitative metrics are used to measure and predict productivity and quality for both software products and processes.
* **Key Process Areas:** Software Quality Management, Quantitative Process Management.

*Level 5: Optimizing* 
* **Characteristics:** The organization is fully focused on continuous process improvement.
* **Key Process Areas:** Process Change Management, Technology Change Management, Defect Prevention.

### Change Control and Configuration Management
**Change Control** 
Change control is the set of procedures that ensure that changes are made only after due consideration of their impact.
Project changes can arise for many reasons, including: 
- Change in the business environment
- Suggestions from the development team
- The availability of new technology, offering different possible system solutions.
- A straightforward change of mind by the users as to what they want. ...

Changes should be analyzed before they are made to the existing system, recorded before they are implemented, reported to have details of before and after, and controlled in a manner that will improve quality and reduce error. 

**Configuration Management** 
Configuration management ensures that, if the changes are implemented, then amendments to each of the affected deliverables are properly controlled and recorded. 
It is the process of identifying and defining the items in the system, controlling the changes to these items throughout their life cycle, recording and reporting the status of items and change requests, and verifying the completeness and correctness of items.
- it answers what, who, when and why changes are made.

***Terms*** 
*Configuration Item*: “Aggregation of work products that is designated for configuration management and treated as a single entity in the configuration management process.”

*Version*: The initial release or re-release of a configuration item associated with a complete compilation or recompilation of the item. 

*Revision*: Change to a version that corrects only errors in the design/code, but does not affect the documented functionality.

*Release*: The formal distribution of an approved version.

*Baseline*: “A specification or product that has been formally reviewed and agreed to by responsible management, that thereafter serves as the basis for further development, and can be changed only through formal change control procedures.”

**Configuration Management Roles** 
*Configuration Manager* Responsible for identifying configuration items. The configuration manager can also be responsible for defining the procedures for creating promotions and releases.

*Change control board (CCB) member* Responsible for approving or rejecting change requests.

**Software Configuration Management Activities** 

- *Configuration Identification* - a process where a system is divided into uniquely identifiable components for the purpose of software configuration management.
	- Every source item should have a status attribute - e.g. untested, module tested and integration tested.
	- A document also can have such values as draft, proposed and approved. 
- *Configuration Control* - this process starts, when a need for change is noticed. 
	- A properly completed change request form is sent to the configuration control board (CCB)
	- if approved, proposed changes are assigned to developers to be implemented. then verified.
- *Configuration Status Accounting* - recording and reporting of information needed to manage a configuration effectively
	- Status accounting reports include change logs, progress reports, CI status reports and transaction logs.
- *Configuration Audit* - A representative from management, the QA department, or the customer usually performs such audits. 
	- Functional Configuration Audit (FCA)  - the software product has been built according to specified requirements, 
	- Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) -  to determine whether all the items identified as a part of CI are present in the product baseline, and
	- SCM system audit or in-process audit - whether defined SCM activities are being properly applied and controlled

*SCM Plan* 
The main purpose of the SCM plan is to answer such questions as: who is going to do what, when, where, and how.
Thus, the SCM plan serves as a guideline for the people working with software configuration management. 
A configuration management plan is written for each project.
However, an organization may use a generic SCM plan template that can be tailored to each particular project.

##### Plan Human resource Management
Human resource planning is used to determine and identify human resources with the necessary skills required for project success. 
The human resource management plan describes how the roles and responsibilities, reporting relationships, and staffing management will be addressed and structured within a project. 
It also contains the *staffing management plan* 

##### Acquire Project team
The process of confirming human resource availability and obtaining the team necessary to complete project activities.
Outlining and guiding the team selection and responsibility assignment to obtain a successful team. 

##### Develop Project team
The process of improving competencies, team member interaction, and overall team environment to enhance project performance. 
It results in improved teamwork, enhanced people skills and competencies, motivated employees, reduced staff turnover rates, and improved overall project performance. 

##### Manage Project team
The process of tracking team member performance, providing feedback, resolving issues, and managing team changes to optimize project performance.
It influences team behavior, manages conflict, resolves issues, and appraises team member performance.

### Project Communications Management
Includes the processes that are required to ensure timely and appropriate planning, collection, creation, distribution, storage, retrieval, management, 

* **Plan Communications Management:** Develop a communication strategy based on stakeholder needs and organizational assets.
* **Manage Communications:** Execute the communication plan by creating, collecting, distributing, storing, and retrieving project information.
* **Control Communications:** Monitor and adjust communications throughout the project to ensure stakeholder information needs are met.

The communication activities involved in these processes may often have many 
potential dimensions that need to be considered, including, but not limited to: 
- *Internal* (within the project) and *external* (customer, vendors, other projects, organizations, the public);
- *Formal* (reports, minutes, briefings) and *informal* (emails, memos, ad-hoc discussions); 
- *Vertical* (up and down the organization) and *horizontal* (with peers); 
- *Official* (newsletters, annual report) and *unofficial* (off the record communications); and 
- *Written* and *oral*, and *verbal* (voice inflections) and *nonverbal* (body language).
